Why the old web felt more alive

A tiny argument for ugly personal websites, weird forums, and making things because you can.

<html>YOUR
OWN
SPACE</html>

There was a particular kind of internet where a person could make a page, put it online, and nobody had to approve it first. It might have blinking text, a MIDI file, a visitor counter, and a background tile that should never have escaped Photoshop.

It was objectively messy. It was also personal. A homepage could be a shrine to a game, a hand-built tutorial, a comic archive, or an inexplicable collection of screenshots. The lack of polish was part of the point.

The interesting part wasn't the nostalgia

The interesting part was ownership. Modern platforms make publishing incredibly easy, but they also make everything look and behave the same. Your post lives inside someone else's interface, next to someone else's metrics.

Maybe the future of the web is not making everything cleaner. Maybe it is giving people more places to be weird.

So here's the experiment

RetroBoard borrows the density of an old imageboard, but keeps the useful modern bits: responsive layouts, readable typography, client-side search, keyboard-friendly controls, and pages that work without a framework.

If this site looks slightly broken on purpose, that is the aesthetic. If it actually breaks, please file a bug. Preferably with an attached screenshot and an unnecessarily detailed description.
